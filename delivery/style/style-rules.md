# Project 42 content style rules

Goes into the context set of **every** content run, per section 8.6 of
`pmo/plans/foundry-powered-project-42.md`.

Five vendor families drafting learner-facing content will drift in voice,
reading level, and terminology unless the rules travel with the prompt. This
file is how they travel.

## Voice

- **Second person.** "You configure the endpoint," not "the user configures."
- **Present tense** for how things work. Past tense only for what happened.
- **Active voice.** Name who does the thing.
- **Plain.** Short sentences. One idea each. No throat-clearing before the
  point.
- **Confident where the evidence is, hedged where it is not.** Never hedge to
  sound modest and never assert to sound authoritative.

## Reading level

Written for a working professional who is competent in their own field and new
to this one. Assume intelligence, do not assume prior AI knowledge.

- Define a term the first time it appears in a module.
- No unexplained acronym, ever.
- A concrete example beats an abstract definition. Give both when the concept
  is load-bearing.

## Structure

- Lead with what the reader will be able to do.
- One concept per section.
- Every code block or command is runnable as written, or explicitly marked as a
  fragment.
- End a module with something the learner does, not something they read.

## Hard rules

- **No em-dashes.** Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop.
- **No exclamation marks** in instructional prose.
- **No "simply", "just", "obviously", "of course".** They tell a stuck learner
  the problem is them.
- **No "AI will revolutionize"** framing. This platform teaches how things
  work, not how exciting they are.
- **No claim without a dated first-party source.**
- **No vendor presented as the default.** Provider-neutral is a product
  commitment, not a preference.

## Accessibility, in the draft not the review

- Every image needs alt text describing what it conveys, not what it shows.
- Never carry meaning by colour alone.
- Every video or animation needs a text-only equivalent.
- Headings nest properly; no level is skipped to get a size.
- Link text says where it goes. Never "click here."

## Bias and the conflict of interest

Project 42 teaches model comparison **using models**. Therefore:

- **Comparative content about a provider is never drafted by that provider's
  model.**
- Comparative claims cite measurements or first-party documentation, never a
  model's opinion of itself or a competitor.
- Content produced with model assistance says so.
